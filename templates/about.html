One building, since 1870

Henriette Hørlücks Skole has stood in the same part of Odense since 1870. In 2011, the school opened an international department — Odense International School — inside the same building, for families who needed an English-medium education without leaving the community their children were already growing up in.

The result is a genuinely shared campus. Assembly happens together. The playground is the same one. But from the first bell of the day, OIS students follow the Cambridge curriculum in English, while their Danish-department classmates follow the Folkeskole system next door.

Learning that starts with a question

Lessons at OIS are built around inquiry rather than recitation — three things we try to get right in every classroom.

Inquiry first

Cross-curricular projects built around real questions, not just the next chapter in a textbook.

Global by default

Forty-plus nationalities in one building means classmates, not case studies, teach the rest of the world.

Small enough to notice

Class sizes stay small enough that a child who's struggling — or bored — doesn't go unnoticed for long.

What inquiry actually looks like

A lesson rarely starts with a definition here. It starts with something children can touch, build, or argue about — and the explanation catches up once they've already asked the question themselves.

Accreditation

OIS is a certified Cambridge International Examinations Centre, which means our curriculum, assessments, and IGCSE results are set and checked against the same standard used by Cambridge schools worldwide.
